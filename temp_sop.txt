3. WHAT WE’RE SEEING FROM LISTENERS
Genre in Spotlight: Romantasy
What tends to make a Romantasy story stronger in audio?
Across the Romantasy titles we’ve been reviewing, a few patterns come up repeatedly:
1. Let the romance build through choice, not just chemistry. 

Supernatural bonds, instant attraction, and physical pull can create intrigue, but they shouldn’t do all the emotional work. Give the leads shared experiences, moments of vulnerability, conflict, and trust-building so the relationship feels earned rather than predetermined.
See it in action: The Alpha She Chose
 What makes this premise interesting is the emphasis on choice rather than predestination. In a genre where fated-mate bonds can bring two characters together automatically, the heroine’s decision becomes part of the relationship itself. The supernatural connection may create the pull, but choosing what to do with that connection gives the romance its emotional stakes.
[ Explore The Alpha She Chose →https://pocketfm.com/show/the-alpha-she-chose/69e2eb3772b49e749fed57ae96a86147dde154cb ]
2. Give the protagonist a goal beyond the relationship.

A strong Romantasy lead should actively shape the plot. Give her something she wants, investigate, protect, or fight for outside the romance, so the love story complicates her journey rather than becoming the whole journey.
See it in action: Last Witch of Swendel
 The protagonist is defined first by who she is and what is at stake for her, rather than by her relationship to a romantic partner. Being the “last witch” creates a larger story around survival, identity, power, and what she stands to protect. That gives the romance another narrative arc to intersect with and complicate.
[ Explore Last Witch of Swendel →https://pocketfm.com/show/96f6721dc499b730f42b02d86e640d682f4f665d ]
3. Make the fantasy world put pressure on the romance.

The magic, politics, rules, and antagonists should create real obstacles for the couple. A forbidden bond, dangerous power, faction conflict, or recurring antagonist can make the relationship harder in a way that is specific to that world, which is what makes the fantasy feel essential rather than decorative.
See it in action: Bride of a Dragon
 Here, the fantasy premise itself shapes the relationship. The heroine isn’t simply falling for someone who happens to exist in a magical world — becoming the bride of a dragon brings questions of power, obligation, and danger directly into the romantic dynamic. Remove the fantasy setup, and you fundamentally change the relationship and its conflict.
[ Explore Bride of a Dragon → https://pocketfm.com/show/bride-of-a-dragon/514efd834428337923e640a1254ea9d7477b18b3]

Vendor Engagement Plan
90-Day Human-Touch Journey (Every 10th Day)
Licensing Team | Post-Sales, Pre-Launch Engagement
A vendor doesn't sign with Pocket FM and then disappear into a queue — behind every contract is a person who just handed us a story that means something to them, and then... silence, until the next official update. That silence is where relationships quietly erode. This plan exists to fill it — not with more process, but with small, genuine moments that remind the vendor a real human at Pocket FM is thinking about them, not just their deliverable.
Because Licensing sits before production, we deliberately never touch recording status, episode counts, or launch timelines — that's not ours to share, and pretending otherwise would break trust the moment the vendor compares notes elsewhere. What we can own, fully, is the relationship: making sure that whatever happens on the production side, the vendor never feels forgotten on ours.
So the rule for every touchpoint below is the same: keep it short — under two minutes for the vendor to read and respond to — but make it unmistakably human. A quick, warm message sent with real attention will always land better than a long, polished one that reads like a template.
What We Want the Vendor to Feel
“Pocket FM is still connected with me — I'm not just a line item in a system.”
“My story matters to them, not just the numbers it might generate.”
“I know exactly whom I can reach out to, and they'll actually reply.”
“I am part of the Pocket FM ecosystem — not just someone who signed a contract and went quiet.”
“Even when I don't hear about production, I never feel forgotten.”
The 90 Days at a Glance
A quick-reference view of the cadence — full detail, sample messages, and the reasoning behind each one follow below.
The Journey, Day by Day
  Day 10  —  Personal Check-in
Why this matters: The first ten days after signing can feel purely transactional — contracts, paperwork, payment timelines. This is the moment to remind the vendor that there's a real person on the other end who genuinely cares how they're doing, not just someone tracking a deal.
What to do: A short 5–10 minute call is best if you can manage it — voice carries warmth that text can't. If a call isn't possible, a personal, unhurried email works just as well. Ask an open question and actually make space for the answer.
Ready-to-send message: 
"Hi [Name], hope you're doing well! I just wanted to personally check in now that we're a couple of weeks into our journey with [Show Name]. How are you feeling about everything so far? And is there anything — big or small — I can help make easier from our side?"
Pro tip: If you spoke during the deal process, reference something specific they said then (“I remember you mentioning you were most excited about ___”). It shows you were genuinely listening, not just closing a deal.
  Day 20  —  Story Behind the Story
Why this matters: Authors love talking about their craft. This is an easy, joyful conversation that costs the vendor almost nothing but makes them feel seen as a storyteller — not a signature on a contract.
What to do: Ask one genuine, specific question about their story. Keep it conversational, not like an interview.
Ready-to-send message: 
"Hi [Name] 😊 Been thinking about [Show Name] and wanted to ask — what first inspired you to write this story? I'd love to hear a little of the story behind the story, if you're up for sharing!"
Pro tip: If they answer, reply with a genuine reaction, not just “thanks!” — a one-line “that's such a lovely reason” goes a long way.
  Day 30  —  Favourite Character
Why this matters: A lighter, almost playful touchpoint — a good reminder that not every message from Pocket FM is transactional or serious.
What to do: Keep it short and fun. This one doesn't need a call — a quick, casual message is perfect.
Ready-to-send message: 
"Quick fun question for you today — out of everyone in [Show Name], which character do you think listeners are going to fall for the most? And honestly... is it your favourite too? 😄"
Pro tip: Remember their answer — if you ever do a community spotlight or shoutout later, mentioning the character they picked is a small, memorable callback.
  Day 40  —  Feedback Pulse
Why this matters: A genuine, judgment-free space to raise concerns before they build up. It also quietly signals that you care about their experience, not just their deliverable.
What to do: One simple, sincere question. Make it easy and safe for them to say something isn't working.
Ready-to-send message: 
"Hi [Name], quick and honest check-in — how has your overall experience with Pocket FM been so far? If anything has felt confusing, slow, or just not great, I'd genuinely love to know so we can fix it."
Pro tip: If they do share a concern, thank them for being honest before addressing it — never get defensive, even if the concern feels unfair.
  Day 50  —  Community Invite
Why this matters: Moves the relationship from one-to-one to belonging — being part of something larger than a single contract.
What to do: Invite them personally; don't just drop a generic link and move on.
Ready-to-send message: 
"Hi [Name], I wanted to personally invite you to our author community — it's where a lot of our Pocket FM authors hang out, swap notes, and sometimes just vent about writer's block 😊 Would love to have you there: [Link]"
Pro tip: If there's an upcoming mixer or event, mention it by name instead of just sharing the community link — specificity always reads warmer.
  Day 60  —  Pocket FM Insider Update
Why this matters: Makes the vendor feel like an insider rather than an outside party — they're hearing news as part of the family, not through a press release.
What to do: Share one genuinely interesting, non-production update — a milestone, a feature, or a relevant case study.
Ready-to-send message: 
"Hi [Name], thought you'd find this interesting — [share news / case study link]. Just wanted to keep you in the loop as someone who's part of the Pocket FM family now!"
Pro tip: Pick something actually relevant to their genre or interests rather than a generic company announcement — relevance is what makes it feel personal.
  Day 70  —  Ask Their Opinion
Why this matters: Flips the usual dynamic — instead of always asking things of the vendor, you're asking for their expertise. That builds real respect.
What to do: Ask a light, opinion-based question about storytelling — not about business or process.
Ready-to-send message: 
"Hi [Name], picking your brain today 😊 As someone who really understands storytelling, what do you think makes an audio story truly addictive for listeners? Genuinely curious what you'd say."
Pro tip: If their answer is interesting, tell them so — “I'll keep that in mind” costs nothing and makes people feel properly heard.
  Day 80  —  Referral Conversation
Why this matters: A natural, non-pushy way to grow the vendor network through trust — best reserved for vendors who've clearly had a warm experience so far.
What to do: Frame the ask as a compliment, never as a sales pitch.
Ready-to-send message: 
"Hi [Name], I've genuinely loved working with you on [Show Name] — it's been such a smooth, lovely partnership. If you happen to know other authors or agencies with stories you think would be a great fit for Pocket FM, I'd love an introduction!"
Pro tip: Only send this if the relationship has visibly been warm so far. If engagement has felt lukewarm, it's fine to skip or delay this one.
  Day 90  —  Appreciation & Recap
Why this matters: Closes the 90-day loop with warmth, not a whimper — and reminds the vendor the relationship isn't ending here, just evolving.
What to do: A heartfelt, personal thank-you note — not a templated closing message.
Ready-to-send message: 
"Hi [Name], hard to believe it's been 90 days since we partnered on [Show Name]! Thank you for trusting Pocket FM with your story — it's genuinely meant a lot working with you. I'm still right here on the licensing side, so please don't hesitate to reach out anytime."
Pro tip: If a small personal gesture feels appropriate — a handwritten-style note, a shoutout — Day 90 is the natural moment for it.
Making It Feel Real, Not Templated
Always use their name, and reference something specific from your last conversation — it's the single biggest difference between “warm” and “corporate.”
Vary the channel. Don't let every touchpoint be an email — mix in calls, a WhatsApp voice note, or even a short personal video message occasionally.
Reply quickly and warmly if they respond. The follow-through matters more than the outreach itself — a fast, genuine reply builds more trust than the original message did.
Keep a short personal note per vendor (a hobby, a detail about their story, something they mentioned once) so future touchpoints can reference something real instead of feeling generic.
Don't rush the message. One extra minute of genuine thought is usually the difference between something that reads as sincere and something that reads as templated.
Guardrails — What to Avoid
Production progress, recording status, or episode-level updates — this sits outside Licensing's scope, and sharing it (even informally) can create confusion or false expectations.
Making every touchpoint about contracts, payments, or process — the vendor should feel like a person to us, not a pending task.
Over-communicating beyond the 10-day cadence, even with good intentions — consistency builds trust faster than frequency.
Promising information or timelines that Licensing does not own, such as exact launch dates.
If the Vendor Doesn't Respond
Don't chase. One gentle, warm nudge at most — then simply move on to the next scheduled touchpoint.
Keep the tone light and low-pressure. This is relationship-building, not status-chasing, and it should never feel like either party owes the other a reply.
Log it as “no response” in the tracker so the pattern is visible over time — some vendors are simply less responsive by nature, and that's fine.
Simple Engagement KPIs
Vendor contact rate (touchpoints sent on schedule)
Response rate
Feedback received
Community / event participation
Vendor referrals / new IP leads
Vendor satisfaction or sentiment
Overall approach: fewer automated updates, more genuine short conversations. Ninety days is a long time to be quiet — but it's also a real opportunity to become the one person at Pocket FM a vendor actually looks forward to hearing from. That's the whole plan.
